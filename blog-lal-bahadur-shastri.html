Lal Bahadur Shastri: Soul of Self-Reliance and Dignity

menu_book
Towards My Bharat Historical Desk Forgotten Heroes & Sovereign Memory Series
share Share
“Lal Bahadur Shastri – The man who gave India its soul of self-reliance and dignity.”
— A Living Reflection on Leadership and Honour

Can you guess the person?

The man who owned nothing, being called one of the poorest prime ministers of India and led the nation through its most crucial time, still doesn’t get the justice he deserved: no showoff, no pretence, just simplicity and dedication.

He gave the nation a very powerful slogan, “Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan” yet died in a foreign land just after signing a peace Agreement.

Yes, he is none other than our second Prime Minister of India — Lal Bahadur Shastri, sharing the same birthday as Mahatma Gandhi, born on 2 October 1904 in Mughalsarai, United Provinces of Agra and Oudh, British India.

His father Sharada Prasad Srivastava was a school teacher [1]. He died when Shastri ji was a child, and his mother, Ramdulari Devi brought him up as a single mother.

Sacrifices for Freedom

An age when youth often ask, “What will I get from this?” But at 16, Shastri asked a completely different question: “What can I give for my country?”

In 1921, at age 16, he left school to join the Non-Cooperation Movement with Mahatma Gandhi on his call. He later studied at Kashi Vidyapith and received the title “Shastri”

At the age of 17, he endured jail imprisonment with other freedom fighters while protesting against the British.

Lal Bahadur Shastri Biography: Political Journey and Leadership

Even as Prime Minister, he was remembered for extraordinary simplicity and personal integrity. It was widely reported that he lived in a small rented house, his great sacrifice and dedication helped India overcome war and hunger crises.

In the mid-1960s, when India faced a shortage of food, he prioritized agriculture and supported fair-price shops. He supported the Green Revolution and the White Revolution, which improved grain and milk production.

His firm decision to retaliate and his quiet determination turned the tide of the India-Pakistan war phase in 1965. Shastri was widely remembered for making a peaceful resolution in that war by signing a declaration at Tashkent, which is also called the Tashkent Declaration, signed by Shastri and Pakistani President Ayub Khan on 10 January 1966 in Tashkent, present-day Uzbekistan.

Death or Mystery

Shastri's death still carries a big mystery around it, though his death was reported on 11 January 1966 just after the declaration was signed in Tashkent.

The official records show it was due to a sudden heart attack, but his sudden death continues to generate speculation to this day, as no postmortem was conducted in the USSR.

help_outline

Unanswered Questions of Tashkent

His wife claims that the death of Shastri ji was due to poisoning. The Russian butler who was arrested and suspected of killing Shastri ji was soon released after doctors certified cardiac arrest as the reason for his death.

So the question persists across generations: was it death or murder?

Shastri ji's life teaches us what true leadership is: courage in crisis, honesty in power, and putting others before self.
